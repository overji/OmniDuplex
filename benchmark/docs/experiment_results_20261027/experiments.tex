\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

We evaluate response decisions with independent video-prefix probes and streaming response timing with realtime inference. Each reference response yields a negative prefix ending one second before the response window and a positive prefix ending at its end. Probe positive/negative accuracy measures whether a model speaks or remains silent; paired accuracy requires both decisions to be correct. Unparseable decisions count as incorrect; payload-format validity is reported separately and does not gate a positive decision. Missing inference records are reported as missing coverage. Realtime recall, precision and F1 use one-to-one onset matching within inclusive annotated windows; timing hits do not require correct content.

On 1,103 shared complete pairs, Gemini-3.7-flash and Qwen3.8Omni achieve paired accuracies of 42.79\% and 26.75\%, respectively. The local MiniCPM and Qwen3 models respond on more than 90\% of positive prefixes, but reject fewer than 15\% of negative prefixes on this subset. Their low paired accuracy therefore reflects difficulty withholding premature responses, rather than an inability to emit a response. Qwen2.5 exhibits the opposite decision imbalance, with low positive accuracy despite high negative accuracy. These decision metrics do not establish whether generated response content is correct.

On the 2,008 shared realtime QA, MiniCPM achieves timing F1 of 25.31\%, compared with 18.47\% for Gander and 14.86\% for JoyAI. Gander has the highest timing precision (38.99\%) but lower recall (12.10\%). JoyAI has the highest recall (26.52\%), accompanied by 11,991 outputs and precision of 10.32\%. This illustrates why window recall should be interpreted together with output precision. JoyAI receives visual input only, so this table is not an input-matched audio-video comparison.

\subsection{Interpretation and Limitations}

The remote model names, Gemini-3.7-flash and Qwen3.8Omni, were confirmed by the operator; the two remote files contain predictions but no run configuration or deployment IDs. Their pair IDs were verified against the exact first-500 JSON export. All 500 QA were uniquely mapped to the local full dataset by full-record fingerprints, correcting 479 shifted sample indices. The shared-subset tables control example identity and completion, but do not establish identical remote prompts, frame sampling, reasoning budgets or generation limits. Cross-model conclusions remain subject to these differences. The first 500 QA are an ordered prefix, not a random representative sample. Shared completed subsets may omit the hardest inputs. Agent call validity measures output format, not successful tool execution. Tools are not executed, and returned tool results are not fed back. The cancelled reference-history trial is excluded throughout. No new model inference or paid judging was performed to construct this report.
